\chapter{Results and Discussion}

This chapter reports the results obtained by applying the pipeline to the six-gene panel, with
$100$ sequences requested per gene (and a per-gene override of $200$ for the data-limited
\emph{algD}), with every thermodynamic quantity computed in $1\times$~PBS at $37\,^{\circ}$C
(Section~\ref{sec:thermo}). It follows the flow of the method: sequence retrieval and
curation, the screening funnel, three-dimensional validation, the diversity and rarefaction
analyses, the effect of the organism-aware parameters, the comparison with the reference panel,
the assembly of probe--graphene models, and the curated dataset of prioritised candidates.

\section{Screening funnel}

Across the six genes, the pipeline generated $7661$ candidate windows and filtered them down to
$2439$ that passed both the thermodynamic screen and the secondary-structure screen
(Table~\ref{tab:funnel}). The GC, melting-temperature and homodimer filters remove the largest numbers
of windows ($1687$, $1609$ and $1403$, respectively), as expected, since they encode the core
hybridisation requirements. The homodimer filter is most severe for \emph{oprL}, \emph{rmpM} and
\emph{algD}, removing $55$, $47$ and $41\%$ of the windows that reach it, and mildest for the AT-rich
\emph{nuc} ($8\%$). In the GC-rich \emph{P.\ aeruginosa} genes, the upper bound of the \acrshort{tm}
window also removes many windows ($583$ of the $1360$ of \emph{oprL} and $368$ of the $1012$ of
\emph{algD}), since G--C pairs raise the stability of the duplex \citep{santalucia2004}; this is the
purpose of that bound, which excludes over-stable probes, and it still leaves $254$ and $288$ candidates
for the two genes. The secondary-structure screen removes only $136$ further windows, confirming that
most structurally problematic candidates are already eliminated by the thermodynamic filters.

\section{Genetic diversity of the targets}

The alignment-free diversity analysis (Table~\ref{tab:diversity}) explains much of the
behaviour observed above. \emph{lytA} is by far the most diverse gene (mean cosine distance
$0.187$ and $93\%$ unique sequences), consistent with its known allelic diversity in
\emph{S.\ pneumoniae} \citep{obregon2002}; this justifies the relaxed conservation threshold
applied to it. At the opposite extreme, \emph{frdB} is the most conserved and most redundant
gene (mean cosine distance $0.025$ and only $35\%$ unique sequences, i.e.\ many exact
duplicates in \acrshort{ncbi}), which is why it yields probes of very high conservation.
\emph{algD} has $100\%$ unique sequences but very few of them, which accounts for its
instability at low sampling and motivates the automatic length-clustering and the per-gene
allowance of more sequences. The cosine and Jaccard distances are complementary --- the former
weighs $4$-mer frequencies, the latter only their presence or absence --- and they concur in
identifying \emph{frdB} as the most conserved and redundant gene, giving an alignment-independent
reading of diversity.

\section{Choice of sampling depth}

The rarefaction analysis showed that both the $4$-mer diversity and the $4$-mer richness
saturate at around $N \approx 50$ sequences for the most demanding gene (\emph{nuc}), and much
earlier for the others. Requesting up to $100$ sequences per gene therefore captures essentially
all of the available diversity with a comfortable margin, while additional sequences add little
new information. The true limiting factor is not this cap but the small number of records
available in \acrshort{ncbi} for the data-limited genes.

\section{Effect of the organism-aware parameters}

The value of resolving thresholds per organism type and species is clearest when scoring the
reference panel, which spans bacteria, viruses, a fungus, a protozoan and a human control. When
the $74$ reference probes were scored with a single global (bacterial) criterion, $33$ passed
the basic screen; when instead each was scored by the criteria of its own species and type,
$42$ passed. The additional probes are precisely those whose genome composition differs from
the balanced bacterial case --- for example the AT-rich \emph{Plasmodium} probe, which the
global GC window would reject but which is appropriate under the protozoan profile. This
confirms that organism-aware thresholds avoid unfair rejections without loosening the criteria
for the balanced targets. The gene-level profiles have the same effect on the designed probes: $10$ of
the $30$ probes finally selected pass the basic screen only because of the profile of their gene ---
four of the five \emph{nuc} probes and three each of the \emph{algD} and \emph{frdB} probes --- and would
have been rejected by the global GC or \acrshort{tm} window although they suit the composition of their
target.

\section{Comparison with the reference panel}

Scored with identical metrics, the designed probes and the reference panel can be compared
directly. Among the probes that pass the basic screen, the designed probes have a slightly higher
mean melting temperature ($62.6$ versus $61.4\,^{\circ}$C) and a very similar mean GC content ($0.49$
versus $0.51$), while the reference probes have a slightly higher mean No-fold score ($84.7$ versus
$81.4$). Because both sets carry a source label and share every column, they can also be
submitted together to Boltz-2, enabling a like-for-like comparison in three dimensions as well as
in sequence space.

\section{Probe--graphene models}
\label{sec:complexres}

All $30$ probes validated in 3D were combined with a graphene sheet to obtain atomistic models of
the sensing interface (Section~\ref{sec:complex}). The predicted probe structures contain between
$359$ and $585$ atoms (median $416$), consistent with single strands of $18$--$28$ nucleotides.

\paragraph{Sizing the sheet.} The lateral dimensions of the sheet were set from the probes
themselves. Measuring, for each predicted structure, its largest extent in the plane gives a
median of $42.0$~\AA{} but a range that reaches $82.9$~\AA{} (Table~\ref{tab:complexes}): the
probes are predicted in a variety of extended and compact conformations, and the most extended are
nearly twice the median. A $4 \times 4$~nm sheet would therefore have been too small for $16$ of
the $30$ probes, whose atoms would overhang the edges --- an artefact that the fit check built
into the assembly script rejects. A $121.6 \times 119.8$~\AA{} sheet ($5700$ carbon atoms) was used
instead, which accommodates all $30$ probes, the most extended one still leaving a margin of
$19.3$~\AA{} on each side.

\paragraph{Assembled models.} The complete set of $30$ probe--graphene models was built, each
comprising $6059$--$6285$ atoms. Table~\ref{tab:complexes} summarises their geometry by gene. Two
observations follow. First, no model contains a steric clash: the closest probe--sheet atom pair
lies between $3.20$ and $3.47$~\AA{} (median $3.25$~\AA{}) across all $30$ systems, that is, within
the physisorption regime reported for nucleobases on graphene \citep{tayo2023} and never below it.
The small spread above the nominal $3.2$~\AA{} separation arises because the probe atom closest to
the surface is generally offset laterally from the graphene atoms beneath it, so the true
interatomic distance slightly exceeds the vertical one.

Second, the probes stand well above the surface: their vertical extent ranges from $20.6$ to
$80.4$~\AA{}. This is relevant to the transduction context of Chapter~2, since in an
electrolyte-gated device the charge carried far from the graphene plane is screened, and only the
portion of the probe within the Debye length contributes appreciably to the signal
\citep{purwidyantri2021}. The models therefore make explicit a property that the sequence-level
metrics cannot capture: probes of identical length may present very different amounts of their
structure close to the sensing surface.

These assembled structures are starting geometries for subsequent docking or force-field
refinement. No interaction energy is computed at this stage, so they support no claim about
binding affinity or preferred orientation; what they establish is that the whole validated panel
can be assembled into clash-free, physically sensible interface models by an automated procedure.

\subsection*{What the models already indicate}

Although the models are unrefined, they make one device-relevant quantity measurable that no
sequence-level metric captures: how much of the probe sits close enough to the graphene to be
electrically visible. In an electrolyte-gated device the signal from charges beyond the Debye
length is screened, and that length depends strongly on the buffer: for the electrolytes
used in a study of DNA detection with graphene transistors it ranges from $0.76$~nm in
$1\times$~PBS to $2.41$~nm in $0.1\times$~PBS and $7.61$~nm in $0.01\times$~PBS
\citep{purwidyantri2021}. Taking the first two as
reference, the fraction of probe atoms lying within the Debye length of the sheet varies markedly
across the panel: from $2.0$ to $14.3\%$ (median $6.9\%$) at $1\times$~PBS, and from $22$ to
$100\%$ at $0.1\times$~PBS. In other words, at physiological ionic strength the great majority of
every probe would be screened, whereas in dilute buffer some probes become almost fully visible and
others remain largely hidden --- a spread that is a property of their predicted conformation, not
of their sequence.

Two consequences follow for the candidates themselves. First, this geometric criterion is
\emph{not} aligned with the 3D confidence used to rank them: across the $30$ models the correlation
between the Boltz-2 confidence and the fraction of the probe within $0.76$~nm is weak and slightly
negative ($r = -0.19$). A probe can therefore be well predicted and still present little of itself
to the sensing surface. Second, and more usefully, the two criteria can be combined. The probe
\emph{p5972} (\emph{algD}) is the only candidate that is simultaneously the best-scoring in 3D
(confidence $0.731$, \textsc{good}) and among the most compact, with a vertical extent of only $20.6$~\AA{} and
\emph{all} of its atoms within $2.41$~nm of the surface; \emph{p2392} (\emph{rmpM}) shows the same
favourable geometry with a more modest confidence. By contrast \emph{p7716} and \emph{p8448}
(\emph{frdB}), despite ranking \textsc{good} in 3D, are extended structures with only $38$ and
$22\%$ of their atoms within $2.41$~nm, so a large part of them would fall outside the sensing
volume.

\subsection*{Coverage by confidence class and by buffer}

Because the panel is already stratified by the Boltz-2 confidence class, the coverage was
tabulated per class and per buffer (Table~\ref{tab:debye_class}), extending the comparison to the
$0.01\times$~PBS electrolyte. Three observations follow. At $1\times$~PBS no model in any class
reaches even $15\%$ coverage, and the class medians ($5.6$, $6.7$ and $7.5\%$ for \textsc{good},
\textsc{moderate} and \textsc{low}) are ordered \emph{against} the confidence, confirming at the
level of the classes what the correlation showed for the individual models. At $0.1\times$~PBS the
models separate: $2$ of $4$ \textsc{good}, $6$ of $11$ \textsc{moderate} and $11$ of $15$
\textsc{low} models place at least half of their atoms inside the screening length, so it is in
this buffer that the criterion is most informative. At $0.01\times$~PBS the criterion saturates:
$29$ of the $30$ models are entirely within $\lambda_D$, the single exception being \emph{p129},
the tallest model of the panel at $83.6$~\AA{} above the sheet, at $91.7\%$. The correlation with
the Boltz-2 confidence remains weak and slightly negative in all three electrolytes ($r = -0.19$,
$-0.26$ and $-0.22$ respectively), so the independence of the two criteria is not an artefact of
the particular buffer chosen.

This analysis carries two important caveats. First, the probe orientation in these models is the
one predicted by Boltz-2, preserved by the rigid-body placement and \emph{not} optimised against the
surface; since single-stranded DNA is known to adsorb flat on graphene through $\pi$--$\pi$
stacking \citep{green2015}, the orientations adopted after docking or dynamics will differ, and
with them the exact coverage values. Second, the fraction of atoms within $\lambda_D$ is a
geometric index derived from the Debye--H\"uckel picture, which overstates the practical penalty of
a concentrated buffer (Table~\ref{tab:inl}). The numbers above should therefore be read as a relative
comparison between candidates and as evidence that interface geometry is an independent axis of
probe quality worth carrying into the selection, rather than as predictions of whether, or how
strongly, a device will respond.

\subsection*{Why $1\times$~PBS is kept as the reference}

These coverage values do not argue for diluting the buffer. Figure~\ref{fig:debyescale} sets the
relevant dimensions side by side. In B-form DNA the base pairs are $0.34$~nm apart along the helix axis
\citep{watsoncrick1953}, so the $18$- to $26$-nucleotide probes selected in this work form duplexes
$6.1$ to $8.8$~nm long, whereas $\lambda_D$ is $0.76$~nm in $1\times$~PBS: even with the duplex
standing on the surface, only about two base pairs lie inside it, and no probe long enough to hybridise
specifically would fit. The mismatch is general for electronic biosensors, in which the Debye length
under physiological conditions is below $1$~nm while a $30$-base aptamer extends up to about $10$~nm
\citep{kesler2020}. Lying mostly outside $\lambda_D$ in $1\times$~PBS is therefore a property of the
transduction rather than of these particular probes, and shortening them would reduce the stability and
specificity of the duplex without bringing it inside.

\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/debye_scale.png}
\end{center}
\caption{Length of a hybridised probe (B-form duplex, $0.34$~nm per base pair \citep{watsoncrick1953})

\label{fig:debyescale}

Several further considerations support keeping $1\times$~PBS as the reference condition. It is the
medium the device is meant to face: its osmolarity and ion concentration approximate those of
physiological body fluids \citep{purwidyantri2021}, so optimising the probes for a diluted buffer would
optimise them for a condition that real samples do not have. Hybridisation is, moreover, still detected
in $1\times$~PBS. In the study from which the Debye lengths are taken, hybridisation of a
$25$-nucleotide target was detected at attomolar concentrations in all three dilutions
(Table~\ref{tab:inl}), and a tenfold increase of $\lambda_D$, from $1\times$ to $0.01\times$~PBS, only
doubled the sensitivity. The Debye length derives from a simplified model of the double layer, valid
only for small potentials \citep{kesler2020}, and the authors of that study attribute the response in
$1\times$~PBS to the contribution of the buffer ions to the ionic density of the Stern layer
\citep{purwidyantri2021}.

Hybridisation itself requires salt: cations screen the electrostatic repulsion between the negatively
charged backbones, so that a higher ionic strength promotes duplex formation \citep{campos2019}. For the
$30$ probes selected here, the median \acrshort{tm} predicted by the same nearest-neighbour model falls
from $58.0\,^{\circ}$C in $1\times$~PBS to $47.0\,^{\circ}$C in $0.1\times$~PBS and to $36.7\,^{\circ}$C
in $0.01\times$~PBS, below the hybridisation temperature of $37\,^{\circ}$C; the last value is an
estimate, since it extrapolates the salt correction to a very low ionic strength. Diluting a sample to
lengthen $\lambda_D$ also dilutes the analyte \citep{kesler2020}. The buffer in which the device is read,
however, need not be the one in which hybridisation takes place: \citet{campos2019} hybridised in a
buffer of high ionic strength ($10$~mM phosphate buffer, $150$~mM NaCl and $50$~mM MgCl$_2$) and measured
in $10$~mM phosphate buffer after a stringency rinse. The probes are therefore designed for
$1\times$~PBS, and the readout buffer remains a parameter of the device protocol.

Strategies that achieve detection at physiological ionic strength act on the device rather than on the
probe sequence: a biomolecule-permeable layer of polyethylene glycol on the sensing surface
\citep{gao2015}, also applied to graphene transistors \citep{gao2016}, or the transduction of a
target-induced conformational change that occurs within the screening length \citep{nakatsuka2018}.
Within the scope of probe design, $1\times$~PBS is thus taken as the physiologically meaningful
reference, and the more dilute buffers as readout scenarios evaluated for sensitivity.

\section{Curated dataset of prioritised candidates}

Finally, the same quality ranking was used to export a larger curated dataset: the top $25$
probes per gene ($150$ probes in total), each with its sequence-level features (\acrshort{tm},
GC, conservation, No-fold and folding energies). This dataset extends the set of $30$ probes
validated in 3D to a broader, biophysically annotated selection of the highest-quality
candidates per gene.
