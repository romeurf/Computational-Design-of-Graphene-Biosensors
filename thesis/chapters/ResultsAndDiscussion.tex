\chapter{Results and Discussion}

This chapter reports the results obtained by applying the pipeline to the six-gene panel, with
$100$ sequences requested per gene (and a per-gene override of $200$ for the data-limited
\emph{algD}), with every thermodynamic quantity computed in $1\times$~PBS at $37\,^{\circ}$C
(Section~\ref{sec:thermo}). It follows the flow of the method: sequence retrieval and
curation, the screening funnel, three-dimensional validation, the diversity and rarefaction
analyses, the effect of the organism-aware parameters, the comparison with the reference panel,
the assembly of probe--graphene models, and the curated dataset of prioritised candidates.

\section{Screening funnel}
\label{sec:funnel}

Across the six genes, the pipeline generated $7661$ candidate windows and filtered them down to
$2439$ that passed both the thermodynamic screen and the secondary-structure screen
(Table~\ref{tab:funnel}). The GC, melting-temperature and homodimer filters remove the largest numbers
of windows ($1687$, $1609$ and $1403$, respectively), as expected, since they encode the core
hybridisation requirements. The homodimer filter is most severe for \emph{oprL}, \emph{rmpM} and
\emph{algD}, removing $55$, $47$ and $41\%$ of the windows that reach it, and mildest for the AT-rich
\emph{nuc} ($8\%$). In the GC-rich \emph{P.\ aeruginosa} genes, the upper bound of the \acrshort{tm}
window also removes many windows ($583$ of the $1360$ of \emph{oprL} and $368$ of the $1012$ of
\emph{algD}), since G--C pairs raise the stability of the duplex \citep{santalucia2004}; this is the
purpose of that bound, which excludes over-stable probes, and it still leaves $254$ and $288$ candidates
for the two genes. The secondary-structure screen removes only $136$ further windows, confirming that
most structurally problematic candidates are already eliminated by the thermodynamic filters.

\section{Three-dimensional validation}
\label{sec:boltz}

From the candidates that passed screening, the five highest-quality per gene ($30$ probes in total)
were selected by the quality score and submitted to Boltz-2, each both as a single strand and as a
duplex with its complementary target. All $60$ predictions completed successfully.

\paragraph{Single strands.} By the overall confidence, $4$ probes were classified as \textsc{good}
($\geq 0.70$), $13$ as \textsc{moderate} ($0.60$--$0.70$) and $13$ as \textsc{low} (below $0.60$),
with confidences between $0.487$ and $0.752$ and a mean of $0.610$. The four \textsc{good} probes
belong to four different genes (Table~\ref{tab:boltz}), so the panel does not concentrate its
best-predicted structures in a single target. These values are modest in absolute terms, which is
expected: the confidence of Boltz-2, like that of AlphaFold~3 on which its metrics are modelled
\citep{abramson2024,wohlwend2024}, is calibrated primarily on proteins, and a short unpaired
\acrshort{ssdna} strand has no single well-defined fold to predict. They are therefore used here as
relative indicators among probes of comparable length, not as absolute measures of structural
correctness.

\paragraph{Duplexes.} Predicted with their complementary targets, the same probes reach
substantially higher confidences, from $0.845$ to $0.949$ (\acrshort{plddt} $0.855$--$0.985$), and
all $30$ fall in the highest class. The contrast with the single strands is itself informative: a
fully complementary duplex has a defined, canonical structure, whereas an unpaired strand does not,
and the model reflects that difference. The predicted duplexes are indeed canonical, with a rise of
$3.20$--$3.43$~\AA{} per base pair (median $3.28$~\AA{}), in agreement with the $3.4$~\AA{} of
B-form DNA \citep{watsoncrick1953}. This is a useful internal check: the structures used in the
interface analysis of Section~\ref{sec:complexres} reproduce the geometry of real DNA rather than an
artefact of the prediction.

\paragraph{Relation to the sequence-level ranking.} The 3D confidence is statistically independent
of the sequence-level quality that was used to select these probes: across the $30$ probes, the
Pearson correlation between the confidence and the quality score is $r = -0.01$, and the confidence
is likewise unrelated to the conservation ($r = 0.05$) and to the No-fold score ($r = -0.10$). The
structure prediction therefore does not restate the sequence ranking: it contributes information of
its own. This is the reason it is carried into the final selection as a separate axis
(Section~\ref{sec:final}) instead of being folded into the quality score, where it would have been
diluted by two signals it does not correlate with.

\paragraph{Reproducibility of the predictions.} Because the change of reference buffer altered part
of the selection (Section~\ref{sec:funnel}), $9$ probe sequences were predicted in both the
preliminary and the final run, which allows the run-to-run variability of Boltz-2 to be measured
directly. For those sequences the confidence changed by $-0.003$ on average, and by at most
$0.052$; the predictions are therefore stable, and differences of this order between probes should
not be interpreted. Over the whole panel, the final selection has a slightly higher mean confidence
than the preliminary one ($0.610$ against $0.603$) and the same number of \textsc{good} probes,
which indicates that screening in the reference buffer did not degrade the structural quality of the
candidates it selects.

\section{Genetic diversity of the targets}

The alignment-free diversity analysis (Table~\ref{tab:diversity}) explains much of the
behaviour observed above. \emph{lytA} is by far the most diverse gene (mean cosine distance
$0.187$ and $93\%$ unique sequences), consistent with its known allelic diversity in
\emph{S.\ pneumoniae} \citep{obregon2002}; this justifies the relaxed conservation threshold
applied to it. At the opposite extreme, \emph{frdB} is the most conserved and most redundant
gene (mean cosine distance $0.025$ and only $35\%$ unique sequences, i.e.\ many exact
duplicates in \acrshort{ncbi}), which is why it yields probes of very high conservation.
\emph{algD} has $100\%$ unique sequences but very few of them, which accounts for its
instability at low sampling and motivates the automatic length-clustering and the per-gene
allowance of more sequences. The cosine and Jaccard distances are complementary --- the former
weighs $4$-mer frequencies, the latter only their presence or absence --- and they concur in
identifying \emph{frdB} as the most conserved and redundant gene, giving an alignment-independent
reading of diversity.

\section{Choice of sampling depth}

The rarefaction analysis showed that both the $4$-mer diversity and the $4$-mer richness
saturate at around $N \approx 50$ sequences for the most demanding gene (\emph{nuc}), and much
earlier for the others. Requesting up to $100$ sequences per gene therefore captures essentially
all of the available diversity with a comfortable margin, while additional sequences add little
new information. The true limiting factor is not this cap but the small number of records
available in \acrshort{ncbi} for the data-limited genes.

\section{Effect of the organism-aware parameters}

The value of resolving thresholds per organism type and species is clearest when scoring the
reference panel, which spans bacteria, viruses, a fungus, a protozoan and a human control. When
the $74$ reference probes were scored with a single global (bacterial) criterion, $33$ passed
the basic screen; when instead each was scored by the criteria of its own species and type,
$42$ passed. The additional probes are precisely those whose genome composition differs from
the balanced bacterial case --- for example the AT-rich \emph{Plasmodium} probe, which the
global GC window would reject but which is appropriate under the protozoan profile. This
confirms that organism-aware thresholds avoid unfair rejections without loosening the criteria
for the balanced targets. The gene-level profiles have the same effect on the designed probes: $10$ of
the $30$ probes finally selected pass the basic screen only because of the profile of their gene ---
four of the five \emph{nuc} probes and three each of the \emph{algD} and \emph{frdB} probes --- and would
have been rejected by the global GC or \acrshort{tm} window although they suit the composition of their
target.

\section{Comparison with the reference panel}

Scored with identical metrics, the designed probes and the reference panel can be compared
directly. Among the probes that pass the basic screen, the designed probes have a slightly higher
mean melting temperature ($62.6$ versus $61.4\,^{\circ}$C) and a very similar mean GC content ($0.49$
versus $0.51$), while the reference probes have a slightly higher mean No-fold score ($84.7$ versus
$81.4$). Because both sets carry a source label and share every column, they can also be
submitted together to Boltz-2, enabling a like-for-like comparison in three dimensions as well as
in sequence space.

\section{Probe--graphene models}
\label{sec:complexres}

All $30$ probes predicted in 3D were combined with a graphene sheet to obtain atomistic models of
the sensing interface (Section~\ref{sec:complex}). The predicted probe structures contain between
$357$ and $529$ atoms (median $403$), consistent with single strands of $18$--$26$ nucleotides.

\paragraph{Sizing the sheet.} The sheet has to hold every probe without overhang, and its lateral
dimensions were therefore set from the probes themselves. The largest in-plane extent of each
predicted structure has a median of 44.6~\AA{} and reaches 67.1~\AA{}
(Table~\ref{tab:complexes}), the probes being predicted in a range of extended and compact
conformations. A $4 \times 4$~nm sheet, a size that would suffice for a nucleotide-scale model,
would have been too small for 19 of the $30$ probes, whose atoms would
protrude beyond the edges --- an artefact that the fit check built into the assembly script rejects
rather than writing a model that is geometrically wrong. The $121.6 \times 119.8$~\AA{} sheet
($5700$ carbon atoms) accommodates all $30$, the most extended still leaving a margin of
27.2~\AA{} on each side.

\paragraph{Assembled models.} The complete set of $30$ probe--graphene models was built in a single
automated run, each model comprising $6057$--$6229$ atoms; Table~\ref{tab:complexes} summarises their
geometry by gene. No model contains a steric clash: across all $30$ systems the closest probe--sheet
atom pair lies between 3.21 and 3.42~\AA{}, that is,
within the physisorption regime reported for nucleobases on graphene \citep{tayo2023} and never
below it. The small spread above the nominal $3.2$~\AA{} separation arises because the probe atom
closest to the surface is generally offset laterally from the graphene atoms beneath it, so the true
interatomic distance slightly exceeds the vertical one. The probes stand well above the surface,
their vertical extent ranging from 23.3 to 59.2~\AA{}; probes of
similar length therefore present very different amounts of their structure close to the sensing
surface, which is the property quantified in Section~\ref{sec:debyeres}.

These assembled structures are starting geometries for subsequent docking or force-field
refinement. No interaction energy is computed at this stage, so they support no claim about binding
affinity or preferred orientation; what they establish is that the whole panel can be assembled into
clash-free, physically sensible interface models by an automated procedure, and that the resulting
models are suitable inputs for the refinement stages that follow.

\section{Why $1\times$~PBS is kept as the reference}
\label{sec:buffer}

These coverage values do not argue for diluting the buffer. Figure~\ref{fig:debyescale} sets the
relevant dimensions side by side. In B-form DNA the base pairs are $0.34$~nm apart along the helix axis
\citep{watsoncrick1953}, so the $18$- to $26$-nucleotide probes selected in this work form duplexes
$6.1$ to $8.8$~nm long (Section~\ref{sec:debyeres} measures the consequence directly), whereas $\lambda_D$ is $0.76$~nm in $1\times$~PBS: even with the duplex
standing on the surface, only about two base pairs lie inside it, and no probe long enough to hybridise
specifically would fit. The mismatch is general for electronic biosensors, in which the Debye length
under physiological conditions is below $1$~nm while a $30$-base aptamer extends up to about $10$~nm
\citep{kesler2020}. Lying mostly outside $\lambda_D$ in $1\times$~PBS is therefore a property of the
transduction rather than of these particular probes, and shortening them would reduce the stability and
specificity of the duplex without bringing it inside.

\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/debye_scale.png}
\end{center}
\caption{Length of a hybridised probe (B-form duplex, $0.34$~nm per base pair \citep{watsoncrick1953})
for the shortest, median and longest probes selected ($18$, $20$ and $26$ nucleotides), compared on the
same vertical scale with the Debye length of the three PBS dilutions \citep{purwidyantri2021}. The dark
segment is the part within $\lambda_D$ in $1\times$~PBS. The geometry is the most favourable one: a
duplex perpendicular to the surface and touching it, without a linker.}
\label{fig:debyescale}
\end{figure}

Several further considerations support keeping $1\times$~PBS as the reference condition. It is the
medium the device is meant to face: its osmolarity and ion concentration approximate those of
physiological body fluids \citep{purwidyantri2021}, so optimising the probes for a diluted buffer would
optimise them for a condition that real samples do not have. Hybridisation is, moreover, still detected
in $1\times$~PBS. In the study from which the Debye lengths are taken, hybridisation of a
$25$-nucleotide target was detected at attomolar concentrations in all three dilutions
(Table~\ref{tab:inl}), and a tenfold increase of $\lambda_D$, from $1\times$ to $0.01\times$~PBS, only
doubled the sensitivity. The Debye length derives from a simplified model of the double layer, valid
only for small potentials \citep{kesler2020}, and the authors of that study attribute the response in
$1\times$~PBS to the contribution of the buffer ions to the ionic density of the Stern layer
\citep{purwidyantri2021}.

Hybridisation itself requires salt: cations screen the electrostatic repulsion between the negatively
charged backbones, so that a higher ionic strength promotes duplex formation \citep{campos2019}. For the
$30$ probes selected here, the median \acrshort{tm} predicted by the same nearest-neighbour model falls
from $58.0\,^{\circ}$C in $1\times$~PBS to $47.0\,^{\circ}$C in $0.1\times$~PBS and to $36.7\,^{\circ}$C
in $0.01\times$~PBS, below the hybridisation temperature of $37\,^{\circ}$C; the last value is an
estimate, since it extrapolates the salt correction to a very low ionic strength. Diluting a sample to
lengthen $\lambda_D$ also dilutes the analyte \citep{kesler2020}. The buffer in which the device is read,
however, need not be the one in which hybridisation takes place: \citet{campos2019} hybridised in a
buffer of high ionic strength ($10$~mM phosphate buffer, $150$~mM NaCl and $50$~mM MgCl$_2$) and measured
in $10$~mM phosphate buffer after a stringency rinse. The probes are therefore designed for
$1\times$~PBS, and the readout buffer remains a parameter of the device protocol.

Strategies that achieve detection at physiological ionic strength act on the device rather than on the
probe sequence: a biomolecule-permeable layer of polyethylene glycol on the sensing surface
\citep{gao2015}, also applied to graphene transistors \citep{gao2016}, or the transduction of a
target-induced conformational change that occurs within the screening length \citep{nakatsuka2018}.
Within the scope of probe design, $1\times$~PBS is thus taken as the physiologically meaningful
reference, and the more dilute buffers as readout scenarios evaluated for sensitivity.

\section{Debye coverage of the interface models}
\label{sec:debyeres}

The models make measurable a device-relevant quantity that no sequence-level metric captures: how
much of the probe, and of its charge, lies close enough to the graphene to be electrically visible
(Section~\ref{sec:debyemethod}). Three geometries were measured --- the rigid placement, the probe
anchored by its $3'$ end, and the anchored duplex --- in the three electrolytes, whose Debye lengths
differ by an order of magnitude.

\subsection{Rigid placement}

In the placement that preserves the predicted orientation, the fraction of probe atoms within
$\lambda_D$ ranges from 2.3 to 14.2\%
(median 7.4\%) in $1\times$~PBS, which corresponds to between
0 and 4 of the
$18$--$26$ phosphates of each probe (median 3). In
$0.1\times$~PBS the fraction rises to 23.2--97.8\%
(median 58.9\%), and in $0.01\times$~PBS it reaches $100\%$ for
every model: the tallest probe stands 62.4~\AA{} above the
sheet, well inside the $76.1$~\AA{} of $\lambda_D$ at that dilution.

Table~\ref{tab:debyeclass} breaks these values down by Boltz-2 confidence class. Three observations
follow. At $1\times$~PBS no model of any class reaches even $15\%$ coverage, and the class medians
are ordered \emph{against} the confidence (\textsc{low} 8.5\%,
\textsc{moderate} 6.2\%,
\textsc{good} 5.6\%). At $0.1\times$~PBS the models
separate, from 23.2\% to 97.8\%, and it is in
this buffer that the criterion is most informative: 22 of
the $30$ models place at least half of their atoms inside the screening length. At $0.01\times$~PBS
the criterion saturates, assigning $100\%$ to every candidate and therefore distinguishing none.

The correlation between coverage and 3D confidence is weak and slightly negative in both buffers in
which it can be computed ($r = -0.19$ at $1\times$~PBS and
$r = -0.14$ at $0.1\times$~PBS; at $0.01\times$~PBS the
coverage is the same for every probe, so no correlation is defined). A well-predicted probe is thus
not a more visible one. This is consistent with the independence already observed between the 3D
confidence and the sequence-level quality (Section~\ref{sec:boltz}), and it is what makes interface
geometry worth carrying as a criterion of its own: it orders the candidates along an axis that
neither the sequence screening nor the structure prediction captures.

\subsection{Anchored probe and hybridised duplex}

The rigid placement inherits the orientation predicted for the free probe, which is arbitrary with
respect to the surface. The anchored geometry removes that arbitrariness by reproducing the
immobilisation used experimentally --- the probe tethered by its $3'$ end
\citep{campos2019,purwidyantri2021} --- and by averaging over the orientations such a tether allows.
Of the $20\,000$ orientations sampled per probe, those retained (median
5339 per probe) place a median of $1$ to $2$ phosphates
within $\lambda_D$ in $1\times$~PBS for 28 of
the $30$ probes, and at most 8 in the most
favourable orientation of any probe. Anchoring therefore reduces the charge inside the screening
length relative to the unconstrained placement, as expected: the probe can no longer present
whichever face happens to be closest to the surface.

The number of retained orientations varies widely between probes, from $98$ to $6331$, and it is
itself informative. It measures how exposed the $3'$ terminus is in the predicted conformation: an
orientation is retained only when that terminus is the part of the molecule closest to the surface,
which is rare when it is tucked into the body of the structure. Across the panel the retained count
is strongly and negatively related to the anchored charge ($r = -0.89$; Spearman $\rho = -0.85$),
and the relation has a geometric reading rather than being an artefact of sampling. When the $3'$
end protrudes, many orientations qualify, but in them the rest of the probe extends away from the
contact point and few phosphates remain within $\lambda_D$. When it is buried, few orientations
qualify, and in those few the molecule is necessarily folded around its own anchor, so more of it
stays close to the surface. The two probes with fewest retained orientations, \emph{p2331} ($98$)
and \emph{p4010} ($542$), are accordingly those with the highest anchored charge ($5$ and $4$
phosphates), while the probes with more than $4000$ retained orientations have a median of $1$.

Two consequences follow. The anchored metric is not a property of the sequence alone: it is governed
largely by where the $3'$ terminus happens to sit in the predicted conformation, which is itself a
prediction. And the estimates for probes with few retained orientations rest on a small sample, so
their values are the least reliable of the panel --- a caveat that applies precisely to the probes
the metric favours.

The hybridised state is the one the device actually senses, since it is the arrival of the target
that changes the charge at the surface. Measured on the anchored duplexes, the result is
unambiguous: in $1\times$~PBS the median number of target phosphates within $\lambda_D$ is zero for
all $30$ probes, and even the most favourable orientation places only
4--7
of them inside. In $0.1\times$~PBS the median rises to $8$ target phosphates, and in $0.01\times$~PBS
the entire target lies inside $\lambda_D$ for every probe. The charge that hybridisation brings into
the sensing volume is thus set by the screening length and by the geometry of the duplex, not by the
length of the probe: a longer probe captures a longer target, but the additional charge is added
beyond $\lambda_D$, where it is screened. This is the quantitative counterpart, within this panel, of
the dimensional mismatch discussed in Section~\ref{sec:buffer}.

\subsection{Final selection}
\label{sec:final}

Because the Boltz-2 confidence and the anchored charge within $\lambda_D$ are independent, the
candidates of each gene were compared on both axes at once and those on the Pareto front retained
(Section~\ref{sec:debyemethod}). The front contains 11 of the $30$ probes
(Table~\ref{tab:final}), between one and three per gene. Its composition shows why neither axis alone
would suffice: it includes the probes with the highest confidence of their gene, such as
\emph{p598} (\emph{nuc}) and \emph{p5178} (\emph{algD}), but also probes of low confidence that
present appreciably more charge at the interface, such as \emph{p4010} (\emph{oprL}) and
\emph{p2331} (\emph{lytA}). Ranking by confidence alone would have discarded the latter; ranking by
coverage alone would have discarded the former.

The two probes admitted on charge alone, \emph{p4010} and \emph{p2331}, are those whose anchored
values rest on the fewest sampled orientations, for the reason given above. Restricting the front to
the $28$ probes with at least $1000$ retained orientations leaves $9$ probes and removes exactly
those two, without changing any other entry. Both lists are defensible, and the difference between
them is reported here rather than resolved: the longer list is the one the stated criterion
produces, while the shorter one is the more conservative reading. Either way, every gene keeps at
least one candidate, and the probes selected on confidence are unaffected.

\subsection{Caveats}

Three limitations bound the interpretation of these values. First, the rigid placement preserves the
orientation predicted by Boltz-2 and is not optimised against the surface; since single-stranded DNA
is known to adsorb flat on graphene through $\pi$--$\pi$ stacking \citep{green2015}, the
orientations adopted after docking or molecular dynamics will differ, and with them the exact
coverage. The anchored geometry is less exposed to this objection, as it averages over orientations
instead of trusting one, but it also idealises the tether: no linker length is added, so the $3'$
end sits as close to the sheet as it can, and the reported coverage is an upper bound. It is,
besides, sensitive to the predicted position of the $3'$ terminus, as shown above.

Second, the coverage is a geometric index derived from the Debye--H\"uckel picture, and that picture
overstates the practical penalty of a concentrated buffer, as the experimental response of the same
devices shows (Section~\ref{sec:buffer}). The values should therefore be used to compare candidates
with one another, which is how they enter the selection, and not to predict whether a device will
respond.

Third, the structures themselves are predictions. The confidences of the single strands are modest
(Section~\ref{sec:boltz}), and although the duplexes are predicted with high confidence and
canonical geometry, no experimental structure of these probes exists against which to verify them.

\section{Curated dataset of prioritised candidates}

Finally, the same quality ranking was used to export a larger curated dataset: the top $25$
probes per gene ($150$ probes in total), each with its sequence-level features (\acrshort{tm},
GC, conservation, No-fold and folding energies). This dataset extends the set of $30$ probes
validated in 3D to a broader, biophysically annotated selection of the highest-quality
candidates per gene.
